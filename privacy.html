Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated: October 20, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Arcana Table (also called ArcanaTable), an independent Magic: The Gathering tabletop app for Android, handles information when you use the app or visit this website. Features and data flows may differ by app version and by whether you use offline, local-network, or internet multiplayer functionality.

1. Information stored on your device

Arcana Table can store player names, game states, card and deck information, preferences, room connection settings, cached card images, and diagnostic logs locally on your device so the app can function and resume play. This data is generally not sent to the developer simply because you use the app offline.

The app does not require you to create a separate Arcana Table account. The reviewed app does not integrate advertising or a dedicated behavioral analytics SDK.

2. Multiplayer and information transmitted over networks

If you choose a multiplayer feature, the app exchanges information needed for your game. Depending on the mode, that may include your chosen player name, room identifiers, connection information, deck status, game-state snapshots, game actions, and in-room chat. Other participants receive information required to display the shared game.

For internet rooms, a separately hosted room service can process and temporarily store room state. The Arcana Table prototype room service uses Cloudflare infrastructure, secure WebSocket connections, and room-password verification. A room password is represented by a hash in its stored room record. A room's game data is intended to be deleted when the last connected player leaves or disconnects, with a 24-hour cleanup fallback. Service-provider operational logs or backups may follow that provider's retention practices.

Local-network games communicate with devices on your network. If you connect to a room service hosted by someone else, that operator and its hosting provider may also process connection metadata and game data under their own practices. Do not place sensitive personal information in player names, chats, or room passwords.

3. Card data, images, and third-party services

When the app requests card details or images, it may contact Scryfall and its image hosts. These requests may reveal technical network information (such as an IP address) and the card being requested to that provider. Images and card-link information may be cached locally to reduce repeat requests.

Optional missing-card requests: If you explicitly use “Request Missing Cards” in the Arcana app, the app sends only the card names you select and a random submission identifier to a dedicated Cloudflare request service. It does not automatically send your whole deck, player name, chat, room details, email, or device identifier. Cloudflare processes normal connection metadata, including the network IP address, to deliver and rate-limit the request. The service uses a keyed, pseudonymous hash of the request's network address for daily abuse limits, and retains selected card names and aggregated request counts so repeats can be combined. Each requested card's name and aggregate request count may appear publicly in the GitHub missing-card tracker; do not put personal information in a card name. Requesting a card does not mean it is supported or that AI development automatically begins. No request is sent unless you deliberately confirm the selected names.

This website is published using GitHub Pages. GitHub may process ordinary website request information according to its own privacy practices. Downloading or purchasing the app through Google Play is separately subject to Google's terms and privacy practices.

4. Closed-test registration

If you choose to sign up for Arcana Table closed testing, we collect the email address you submit and use it to contact you about testing invitations, access, and important test-related updates. We do not use the signup list for unrelated marketing. Submitting an address does not grant automatic access to Google Play testing.

Registration is handled through Google Forms. The Arcana Table website links to the form and does not itself collect the email address you enter. Google processes your submission and relevant technical information in accordance with its privacy practices; the developer can access responses to coordinate beta invitations. By submitting a request, you agree to receive closed-test invitations and essential testing updates. Google's Privacy Policy also applies.

5. Diagnostics and support requests

The app can save diagnostic events and errors on the device. Its diagnostic export feature attempts to redact room secrets, addresses, and private game details, but no automatic redaction is guaranteed to be perfect. Diagnostic files are not automatically submitted to the developer by that feature. If you choose to share an exported log or contact the developer for support, the information you provide can be used to investigate and respond to your request. Review files before sharing them.

6. How information is used and shared

Information is used to run the game, synchronize a multiplayer session, display card content, retain local preferences, troubleshoot issues, and respond to requests that you initiate. Game information may be transmitted to other players in your room and to the infrastructure providers needed to deliver the selected feature. Arcana Table does not sell personal information or use it for targeted advertising.

Information may also be disclosed when required by law or necessary to protect the service, users, or others. Third-party services may handle network metadata under their own policies.

7. Retention and deletion

  • On-device data: saved game data, settings, diagnostics, and caches remain until cleared by the app, removed through Android app settings, or deleted by uninstalling. Android device backup settings may preserve some app data in backups.
  • Internet room data: the project's prototype room service is designed to remove room records after all participants disconnect, with a 24-hour fallback cleanup. Other hosts and infrastructure providers may have different retention practices.
  • Closed-test registration: signup emails are retained while needed to arrange and operate testing or until you request removal. Google may have separate retention practices.
  • Information sent for support: communications and attachments may be kept as needed to address the request and maintain a support record. You may request deletion of personal information you submitted by using the contact method below, subject to legal or security obligations.

Because Arcana Table does not require a separate user account, there is no Arcana Table account to delete. To remove locally stored app data, use Android Settings → Apps → Arcana Table → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app. This may erase saved decks, settings, and progress.

8. Security

Internet room connections are designed to use encrypted WSS transport, and the prototype service applies room-password checks and per-player game-state filtering. Local data is subject to your device's security and backup settings. No system can guarantee absolute security; room passwords should be unique and should not be reused from other services. Local-network and third-party-hosted rooms may have different security characteristics.

9. Children's privacy

Arcana Table is a general-audience tabletop gaming project and is not specifically directed at children under 13. It does not intentionally solicit personal information from children. If you believe a child has submitted personal information to the developer, use the contact method below to request its removal.

10. Changes to this policy

The policy may be updated as the app and its services evolve. Changes will appear on this page with a new update date. Please review it when using a new version or feature.

11. Contact and privacy requests

Arcana Table is maintained by the independent developer behind the AARMOU/arcana_table project. For privacy questions or data requests, open a project issue on GitHub and identify it as a privacy inquiry. If you applied for closed testing and received an invitation, you can also reply to that email to request removal from the list. GitHub issues are public: do not post passwords, private logs, email addresses, or other sensitive information. For a request involving sensitive data, ask for a private contact channel before providing details.
